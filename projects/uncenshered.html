#UncensHERed

#UncensHERed is a testimony platform and digital collective designed to bridge the gap between individual experiences and public awareness, offering a secure space for users to document platform censorship.

Year: Spring 2026 (4 weeks)
Built for: IAT 438 - UX Design
In collaboration with: Yan W. / Francesca R.
Role: UX Researcher & Visual Designer
Skills & Tools: Research / Storyboard / Figma / Prototype

the Problem

Women's health topics, keywords, and imagery are disproportionately subjected to algorithmic suppression across major social media platforms.

→

the Objective

How might we make of women's health content suppression visible and interpretable to those affected by it?

My Roles

the Process

qualitative interviews +
ethnography

Conducted semi-structured interviews and online ethnographic research to understand how different cultural backgrounds influence the perception and stigma of female health topics.

persona + journey mapping

I synthesized the research findings into 2 distinct personas and mapped their emotional journeys using the website through simple storyboards.

wireframing

Ideation of the first version of the wireframe. Which had led the team to thinking about the user flow of the website and how to effectively provide testimony submission processes.

the Challenges

  1. Creating a movement that unites rather than polarizes people into groups.
  2. Figuring out how users could re-upload censored content to social media without being flagged again to spread awareness.

the Solutions

I led the visual design and structural layout of the platform, focusing on trust, clarity, and impact:

  1. Testimony Engine: a secure, guided submission flow for users to upload screenshots of their suppressed posts and tag the specific platforms.
  2. Informed Consent: a transparent onboarding sequence that explains how user data and stories will be utilized, ensuring informed consent at every interaction point.
  3. Data Visualization: the platform aggregates individual testimonies into a collective data visualization.

the Outcomes

An extention of CensHERship's website for users to share their experience of platform censorship and self-censoring.

the Takeaways

As the current solution successfully addresses CensHERship's testimony collection challenge, if there were more time, I would look into user testing with women's health influencers and organizations to test the effectiveness and efficiency of the prototype.

Overall, I learned two main aspects from this project that has had a deep impact on how I think about design:

  1. Design for Humans: This project reinforced that data points are actual people. I learned the critical importance of seeking out diverse, intersectional perspectives to avoid building within an echo chamber.
  2. The "Third Eye": Working on a social project taught me the necessity of external critique. Iterating based on outside feedback ensured the final platform genuinely served the community's needs rather than just the designers' assumptions.
